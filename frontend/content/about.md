## The range

Bouncing between the two ends is the point, not a lack of focus. The same
question shows up at both:

- Writing compilers — what a language actually promises the machine
- Simulating CPU schedulers — how fairness and throughput trade against each other
- Architecting cloud sandboxes — isolation that holds while staying cheap to start
- Reading theoretical physics — the habit of taking a model seriously enough to break it

## Open questions

What I'm actually working on, stated as the question rather than the answer,
because that's the honest form for all of it.

**Transformers.** Attention is a differentiable lookup, and depth turns lookups
into composition. So which computations does a fixed-depth stack genuinely
admit, and which are only reachable by spending tokens to think in? The
boundary between what the architecture can represent and what training finds is
where I keep ending up.

**Neural network theory.** Scaling laws are the most reliable empirical facts we
have about learning and among the least explained. A power law usually means
something is scale-free — so what is, here? Relatedly: the kernel picture is a
clean theory of networks that don't learn features, which is to say not the
interesting ones. What replaces it?

**Moving definitions between fields.** Take something everyone agrees on — a
derivative, a distance, an average — and rewrite it in a neighbouring field's
language. Most of the time nothing survives the trip, and that's fine; the shape
of the failure is usually the part worth keeping. This is the corner of the
garden with the most dead ends in it, and the one I come back to most.
